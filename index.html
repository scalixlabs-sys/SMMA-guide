Module 01

Campaign setup

The architecture of your campaign determines who sees your ad and how Meta optimizes. Get this wrong and no creative will save you.

Campaign objective

Objective to useLeads
Why not Traffic?Optimizes for clicks, not form fills. You pay for curiosity, not intent.
Why not Conversions?Requires 50+ conversions/week to exit learning. You don't have that yet.
Lead capture typeWebsite (your landing page), NOT Meta Instant Form
Use website leads, not Meta Instant Forms. Instant Forms auto-fill user data — the lead does almost no work, so intent is lower. Someone who navigates to your page and manually fills a form is 2–3× more serious.

Campaign structure

1 Campaign — named for your client e.g. "Leads — [Client Name]"
2–3 Ad Sets — each tests a different audience segment
2–3 Ads per Ad Set — each tests a different creative or copy angle

This is a testing structure. The goal in weeks 1–2 is finding which audience + creative combination produces the lowest cost per lead. Then you scale what works.

Upload your leads spreadsheet as a Custom Audience, then build a 1–2% Lookalike. Only viable after 50+ leads collected. Don't build this on day one.

Module 03

Ad creative

Creative is the #1 performance variable. Perfect targeting with weak creative will fail. This is where most beginners under-invest.

Creative hierarchy

Test in this order. The first two are your priority.

Before/after photos — highest converting format for home services. Real jobs, real transformations. Get these from your client before launching.
UGC-style video — contractor or customer speaking to camera. Unpolished. Authentic. Consistently outperforms produced video.
Problem/urgency image — damaged property, visible issue. Triggers fear and urgency. Works well for emergency or seasonal campaigns.
Testimonial screenshot — screenshot of a Google review on a colored background. Social proof in the feed.
Price-anchored offer — "Free inspection + estimate" with bold text. Works for entry-level lead capture.

Module 04

The learning phase

The most misunderstood part of Meta Ads. Most beginners panic and kill campaigns here. Understanding this will save your campaigns.

Learning phase active

When you see this status in Ads Manager, Meta is still figuring out who to show your ad to. Results will look bad. This is completely normal. The worst thing you can do is make changes.

What is the learning phase?

When you launch a new campaign, ad set, or make a significant change, Meta enters a learning phase. During this period, Meta's algorithm is actively experimenting — it's showing your ad to different types of people in different placements at different times to understand who is most likely to complete your goal (a form submission).


Until Meta collects enough data, its delivery is inefficient. CPMs are higher, CPLs are inflated, and performance looks inconsistent. This is not your campaign failing — it's the algorithm warming up.

How it works

You launch the campaign — Meta begins delivery with no prior data about your specific audience and offer.
Meta experiments — It tests different audience subsets, placements, times of day, and user behaviors. Each impression and click is a data point.
Conversion signal collected — Every time someone submits your form, Meta records who they are. This is the most valuable signal.
Pattern emerges — After enough events, Meta recognizes what a likely converter looks like and begins prioritizing those people.
Learning phase exits — Ads Manager shows "Active" instead of "Learning." Delivery becomes more efficient, CPL stabilizes and usually drops.

The 50-event threshold

Events needed~50 optimization events per ad set per week
What counts as an eventA form submission on your landing page (with pixel installed)
What doesn't countClicks, page visits, video views — only conversions count
With $7/day budgetAt $20 CPL, you get ~10 leads/month — never exits learning
With $30/day budgetAt $20 CPL, ~45 leads/month — close to exiting
Low budgets trap you in learning phase. If you can't hit 50 events per week, consider using ABO (Ad Set Budget Optimization) with one consolidated ad set rather than splitting budget across three — more events in one place exits learning faster.

Learning limited

Sometimes Ads Manager shows "Learning Limited" instead of "Active." This means Meta doesn't think it has enough data to optimize confidently.


Cause 1Audience too small or too narrow
Cause 2Budget too low to generate enough events
Cause 3Too many ad sets splitting the budget
FixBroaden audience, increase budget, or consolidate ad sets

What resets the learning phase

Any of these actions fully reset the learning phase — you lose all collected data and start over:

What does NOT reset learning

Module 05

Marketing psychology

Understanding why people click and fill out forms lets you engineer those decisions deliberately. These are the levers behind every effective ad.

The purchase type

Roofing is a fear-based purchase, not a desire-based one. Nobody wakes up excited to replace their roof. They do it because they're worried — about storm damage, leaks, home value, insurance deadlines. Your ads must speak to the fear first, then offer relief.

The 6 triggers you control

1. Loss aversion

People fear losing $10,000 more than they want to gain $10,000. Frame delay as the cost. "A small repair today prevents a $12k interior water damage claim tomorrow." The risk of inaction must feel real and expensive.

2. Urgency and scarcity

"Only 3 free inspection slots left this week." Creates FOMO. Must feel real or it backfires. Seasonal urgency ("before storm season") is more credible than arbitrary slot counts. Tie urgency to real external events when possible.

3. Social proof

Star ratings, review counts, job photos. Reduces perceived risk. The prospect thinks: "If 400 others trusted them, I can too." Show it prominently in both the ad and landing page. Numbers are more believable than adjectives.

4. Authority and specificity

"Licensed, insured, 12-year certified contractor." Specifics signal legitimacy. Vague claims like "best in the area" are ignored. Specific claims are believed — use certification names, years in business, exact review counts.

5. Friction reduction

Every form field is a reason to quit. You're asking for name, phone, email, job type, budget, and urgency — that's a lot. Your landing page copy must justify each field with a value exchange: "so we can match you with the right specialist." The CTA must reduce perceived effort: "Get my free inspection" beats "Submit."

6. Pattern interrupt

Your ad has 1.5 seconds to stop a scroll. The hook — first line of copy or first video frame — must be unexpected, direct, or visually jarring. "Is your roof lying to you?" stops faster than "Get a free quote today." Familiarity is invisible. Surprise is sticky.

Message match

If your ad says one thing and your landing page says another, you've broken message match. The user feels deceived and bounces. This is responsible for 40–60% conversion rate drops.

Ad headline"Free Same-Day Roof Inspection in [City]"
Landing page H1"Get Your Free Same-Day Roof Inspection"
CTA button"Claim My Free Inspection"

Same language, same offer, continuous commitment. Every step must feel like the natural next move of the one before it.

The funnel psychology

Ad impression — They're not looking for you. You interrupt. Fear or curiosity must hook them in 1.5 seconds or they scroll on.
Click to landing page — The ad promised something. The page must immediately deliver on that exact promise. Any disconnect and they bounce.
Reading the page — They're interested but skeptical. Proof, credentials, and simplicity keep them engaged. Complexity kills momentum.
Starting the form — A micro-commitment. Once started, most people finish. Make the first field easy (name) before asking for phone or budget.
Submitting — They self-qualified. They're a warm lead. Now the contractor must call within 5 minutes — speed at this stage is more important than almost anything else.

Tool 02

Diagnose metrics

When a number looks off, find the symptom here. Each one tells you exactly what to fix and the logic behind why.

Low CTR
CTR below 1% — your ad isn't stopping the scroll
Fix: Your hook isn't compelling. Change the image or the first line of copy. Try fear-based hooks. Don't touch the audience yet — test new creative first. Low CTR is always a creative problem, not an audience problem (unless your audience is wildly wrong).
High CPL
CPL above $60 — something in the funnel is leaking
If CTR is fine but CPL is high: your landing page is the problem. Check message match, form length, and mobile load speed.
If CTR is also low: fix the creative first, then reassess CPL — the two problems are connected.
Low conv. rate
Conversion rate below 2% — clicks aren't turning into leads
Fix: Landing page problem. Check: (1) does the page headline match the ad exactly? (2) Is the form too long or confusing? (3) Does the page load under 3 seconds on mobile? (4) Is your CTA button specific — "Get my free inspection" not "Submit"?
High CPC
CPC above $4 — you're paying too much per click
Fix: Audience is too competitive or too narrow. Try broadening — remove interest filters and let Advantage+ run. Also check if your creative relevance is low, which inflates CPM and therefore CPC.
High frequency
Frequency above 3 — same people seeing the ad too often
Fix: Expand your audience radius, or introduce a new creative. Don't kill the ad set — just refresh what you're showing. Above frequency 5, performance will actively decline and brand perception suffers.
Junk leads
Leads are filling the form but are unqualified or fake
Fix: Add a qualifying question (budget range, property ownership). Check if Audience Network placement is on — exclude it, as it attracts low-quality traffic and click farms. Also verify the homeowner behavior filter is active in targeting.
Good CPL, no jobs
Great leads but the client isn't booking jobs
This is not your ad problem. This is a sales problem on the client's end. The most common cause: slow follow-up. A lead called within 5 minutes closes at 8–10× the rate of one called after 3 hours. Have this conversation directly and clearly with your client.
Learning phase
Ads Manager shows "Learning" status and results look bad
Do nothing. This is expected. Meta needs ~50 conversion events per ad set to optimize. Results look inflated during learning. Wait 7–10 days. See the Learning Phase module for a full breakdown.
Spend not delivering
Budget isn't being spent — delivery is low
Fix: Audience is too narrow for your budget level. Broaden the radius, remove restrictive filters, or check for policy flags on your ad (Meta may have restricted delivery). Also confirm the ad is approved and not stuck in review.

Module 06

Scaling guide

Scaling is not just spending more money. Spending more on a broken system loses money faster. Scale only when you have proof.

The 3 conditions before you scale

Only scale when all three are true:
1. CPL is consistent under $50 for 2+ weeks in a row
2. Your client has confirmed jobs are being booked from your leads
3. The learning phase has exited — Ads Manager shows "Active"

How to scale

Step 1 — Vertical scaling — Increase budget by max 20% every 3–4 days. Going from $50 to $200/day overnight resets the learning phase. Boring, incremental increases are the correct method.
Step 2 — Horizontal scaling — Duplicate your best-performing ad set and increase the budget on the copy. The original keeps running. This avoids resetting learning on a proven campaign.
Step 3 — Lookalike audiences — Once you have 50+ leads in your sheet, upload them as a Custom Audience and build 1%, 2%, and 3% Lookalikes. These are your highest-intent cold audiences.
Step 4 — Retargeting — Create an ad set targeting page visitors who didn't submit. Budget $5–$10/day. Use a different angle: "Still need that free inspection?" Expect CPL 50–70% cheaper than cold traffic.
Step 5 — Add a second client — Once this system is producing consistent results, replicate the setup for a second client. The second campaign takes 20% of the setup time of the first.

Signs you should NOT scale yet

CPL above $60Fix first
Still in learning phaseWait
Client not following up leadsAddress first
Conversion rate below 2%Fix landing page
Frequency above 5Refresh creative first

Long-term client growth

Client 1 — prove the system. Get 1 job booked. Now you have a case study.
Clients 2–3 — sell with results. "I got [Name] 18 leads in 30 days, 4 jobs booked." That closes cold calls fast.
Clients 4–5 — systemize. Use the same campaign template. Your time per client drops significantly.
Beyond — recurring monthly revenue from a portfolio of clients, each on performance-only deals.
